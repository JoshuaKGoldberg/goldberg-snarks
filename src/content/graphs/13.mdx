## Symmetries

A _symmetry_, or _automorphism_, of a graph is a rearrangement of its vertices in which vertices joined by an edge remain joined by an edge.
There are 52 symmetries of <Snark n={13} />.
Each is a combination of:

- a rotation by any number of blocks, for which there are 13 choices;
- a reflection, or no reflection; and
- an exchange of the outer and middle rings, or no exchange.

There are 13 × 2 × 2 = 52 combinations.
There are no others.
Every block contains two cycles of length 5 that share a path, and no other two cycles of length 5 do (<Ref id="short-cycles" />), so a symmetry takes blocks to blocks.
Neighboring blocks stay neighbors, so there are 2 × 13 ways for it to rearrange the blocks around the ring.
If it leaves every block in place, it leaves every _a_ and _b_ in place, and either leaves _c_ and _d_ in place or exchanges them.
That choice determines where the rest of the block goes, as _e_ and _h_ are linked to the next block and _f_ and _g_ to the previous one, and the links then determine every other block.

The same is true of every Goldberg snark, so <Snark n="k" /> has 4*k* symmetries.

## Exchanging the outer and middle rings

Exchanging _c_ with _d_, _e_ with _h_, and _f_ with _g_ in every block is a symmetry, as vertices that were joined by an edge are still joined by one.
It moves the outer ring to the middle ring and the middle ring to the outer ring, and every vertex stays in its block.
