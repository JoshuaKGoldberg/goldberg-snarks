## Diameter

The _distance_ between two vertices is the number of edges in a shortest path between them, and the _diameter_ of a graph is the largest distance between two of its vertices.
The diameter of <Snark n={11} /> is 11.
No other Goldberg snark has a diameter equal to its number of blocks.

<Highlight
	blocks={11}
	kind="farthest"
	label="The Goldberg snark G11, with a path of eleven edges from one side to the other emphasized. It goes in to the inner ring, around half of it, and back out."
>
	A shortest path between two vertices at distance 11.
</Highlight>

A shortest path between vertices in distant blocks follows the outer ring, the middle ring, or the inner ring.
The outer and middle rings take about two edges per block.
The inner ring takes one edge per block, but a path may need up to three edges to reach it and three to leave it.

In <Snark n={5} />, <Snark n={7} />, and <Snark n={9} />, the vertices that are farthest apart are joined by a shortest path along the outer or middle ring, and the diameter is one more than the number of blocks: 6, 8, and 10.
In <Snark n={11} />, no two blocks are more than five apart, so a path along the inner ring has at most <span class="nowrap">3 + 5 + 3 = 11</span> edges.
The same argument gives at most <span class="nowrap">(_k_ + 11)/2</span> for larger Goldberg snarks, and their diameters have been computed to be exactly that up to <span class="nowrap">_k_ = 25</span>.
The diameters of <Snark n={13} /> and <Snark n={15} /> are 12 and 13.

## Crossing number

The _crossing number_ of a graph is the smallest number of edge crossings in any drawing of it.
The crossing number of <Snark n={11} /> is 22, and no larger Goldberg snark has a known crossing number <Cite id="weisstein" />.
