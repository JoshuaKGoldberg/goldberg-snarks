## Coverings

The blocks of <Snark n={15} /> can be numbered from 1 to 5 three times, in order around the ring.
Merging the blocks that have the same number gives [<Snark n={5} />](/g5), so three blocks of <Snark n={15} /> correspond to each block of <Snark n={5} />.
Every edge of <Snark n={15} /> corresponds to an edge of <Snark n={5} />, and the three edges at a vertex correspond to the three edges at its counterpart.
A graph that corresponds to another in this way is called a [_covering graph_](/g9#covering) of it.
The Goldberg snark <Snark n={15} /> is the smallest that is a covering graph of another Goldberg snark.

Since 15 is also a multiple of 3, <Snark n={15} /> is a covering graph of the Goldberg graph [<Snark n={3} />](/g3), which is not a snark.
Each block of <Snark n={3} /> corresponds to five blocks of <Snark n={15} />.

## Chinese remainder theorem

If the blocks of <Snark n={15} /> are numbered from 0 to 14, those of <Snark n={3} /> from 0 to 2, and those of <Snark n={5} /> from 0 to 4, then block _i_ corresponds to the block of <Snark n={3} /> whose number is the remainder of _i_ divided by 3, and to the block of <Snark n={5} /> whose number is the remainder of _i_ divided by 5.
By the Chinese remainder theorem, no two of the numbers from 0 to 14 have the same pair of remainders, so a block of <Snark n={15} /> is determined by the two blocks that it corresponds to.
