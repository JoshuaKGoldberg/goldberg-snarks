## The only heptagon

Look through any Goldberg snark for cycles and you'll find some of five edges, some of six, and some of eight.
There are never any of seven, with one exception.

<Highlight
	blocks={7}
	kind="hub"
	label="The Goldberg snark G7, with the heptagon in its center emphasized."
>
	The hub of <Snark n={7} /> is a cycle of seven edges, and no Goldberg snark has another.
</Highlight>

It's because short cycles come in two kinds.
Some stay within a few neighboring blocks, so they look the same in every ring that's large enough.
The rest go all the way around: the hub does so in as few edges as there are blocks, and every other way takes at least ten.
None of the first kind have seven edges, so a Goldberg snark only has a cycle of seven if its hub is one.

## The cycles that every block has

Each block has two pentagons, which share the path from _c_ through _b_ to _d_.
Leave out _b_ and go around both of them, and that's a hexagon.
There are no other cycles within a block.

<Highlight
	blocks={7}
	kind="block-cycles"
	label="The Goldberg snark G7, with a pentagon in its first block, from b through c, e, f, and d, and a hexagon in its third block, around c, e, f, d, h, and g, each emphasized."
>
	One of the fourteen pentagons, at the top, and one of the seven hexagons, at the right.
</Highlight>

Leaving a block by one link and returning by another costs at least eight edges, which is why the next cycles after those are so much longer.
With seven blocks, that comes to fourteen pentagons, seven hexagons, one heptagon, and 42 cycles of eight.
It's the first Goldberg snark whose only pentagons are its blocks' own.
