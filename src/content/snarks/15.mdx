## Five threes, three fives

Like [<Snark n={9} />](/g9) before it, <Snark n={15} /> wraps around a smaller member of its family.
Unlike <Snark n={9} />, it does so in two ways, as fifteen is both five times three and three times five.

Those two numbers are enough to say where you are.
Tell a friend which block of [<Snark n={3} />](/g3) you're above and which block of [<Snark n={5} />](/g5) you're above, and they'll know which of the fifteen blocks you're on.
That's the Chinese remainder theorem, seventeen centuries old, as a walk around a graph.

## And so on

There's nothing special about stopping here.
Every odd number from five up has a Goldberg snark, and any number that's a multiple of another wraps around it.
With 120 vertices and 180 edges, <Snark n={15} /> is only about where a drawing this size stops being readable.
