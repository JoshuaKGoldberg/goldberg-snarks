## Five threes, three fives

Like [<Snark n={9} />](/g9) before it, <Snark n={15} /> wraps around [<Snark n={3} />](/g3).
Unlike <Snark n={9} />, it also wraps around [<Snark n={5} />](/g5), as fifteen is both five threes and three fives.
That makes it the smallest Goldberg snark that wraps around one that [everybody agrees is a snark](/g3).

Those two numbers are enough to say where you are.
Tell a friend which block of <Snark n={3} /> you're above and which block of <Snark n={5} /> you're above, and they'll know which of the fifteen blocks you're on.
That's the Chinese remainder theorem, well over 1,500 years old, as a walk around a graph.

## And so on

There's nothing special about stopping here.
Every odd number from five up has a Goldberg snark, and whenever one number is a multiple of another, its graph wraps around the other's.
With 120 vertices and 180 edges, <Snark n={15} /> is only about where a drawing this size stops being readable.
