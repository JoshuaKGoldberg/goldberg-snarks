## Eleven steps across

The _diameter_ of a graph is how many steps apart its two farthest vertices are.
The diameter of <Snark n={11} /> is eleven: the same as its number of blocks.
No other Goldberg snark's is.

<Highlight
	blocks={11}
	kind="farthest"
	label="The Goldberg snark G11, with a path of eleven edges from one side to the other emphasized. It goes in to the hub, around half of it, and back out."
>
	A shortest path between two vertices that are as far apart as any. It takes eleven steps.
</Highlight>

It's a race between two routes.

Going around by the outer or middle ring takes about two steps per block, and never has to pass more than half of them.
In small Goldberg snarks, the farthest vertices are nearest by that route, and one more step apart than there are blocks: five blocks are six steps across, seven are eight, and nine are ten.

Going through the hub takes up to three steps in and three back out, but only one for each block passed.
At eleven blocks, the hub wins.
Three steps in, five around, and three out make eleven.

From there, every two blocks more add only one step.
Thirteen blocks are twelve steps across, fifteen are thirteen, and the diameter never catches up again.

## The end of the record

On a flat page, <Snark n={11} /> can't be drawn with fewer than 22 pairs of its edges crossing.
It's the largest Goldberg snark for which that fewest number is on record <Cite id="weisstein" />.
