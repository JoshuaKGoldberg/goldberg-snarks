## Where the family begins

When someone draws "the" Goldberg snark, it's nearly always this one.
Five is the smallest odd number of blocks whose hub isn't a triangle, so <Snark n={5} /> is the first that everybody agrees is a snark.

It's also the only Goldberg snark whose hub is a pentagon.
Every block brings two pentagons of its own, which makes ten, and the hub is the eleventh.

<Highlight
	blocks={5}
	kind="hub"
	label="The Goldberg snark G5, with the pentagon in its center emphasized."
>
	The hub of <Snark n={5} /> is its eleventh pentagon.
</Highlight>

It's sturdy, too.
To cut <Snark n={5} /> into two parts that both still have cycles, you'd have to cut at least five edges, which is one more than snarks are required to withstand.

## Just out of reach

As of 2026, computers have listed every snark of up to 38 vertices, by [the definition used here](/#snark).
There are 1,051,945,967 of exactly 38, and listing those took decades' worth of computing time <Cite id="brinkmann2026" />.
Cubic graphs always have an even number of vertices, so the next size up is 40, which is the size of <Snark n={5} />.
Nobody knows just how many other snarks are as large as it is.
