## Covering

Number the blocks of <Snark n={9} /> in order around the ring: 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3.
Merging the blocks that have the same number gives the Goldberg graph [<Snark n={3} />](/g3), which is not a snark.

<Highlight
	blocks={9}
	kind="every-third"
	label="The Goldberg snark G9, with every third of its nine blocks emphasized."
>
	The three blocks of <Snark n={9} /> that correspond to one block of <Snark n={3} />.
</Highlight>

This makes <Snark n={9} /> a _covering graph_ of <Snark n={3} />.
Each vertex of <Snark n={3} /> corresponds to three vertices of <Snark n={9} />, and the three edges at each of those correspond to the three edges at the vertex of <Snark n={3} />.
The triangle in <Snark n={3} /> corresponds to the inner ring of <Snark n={9} />, which has nine vertices.

In general, <Snark n="m" /> is a covering graph of <Snark n="k" /> whenever _m_ is a multiple of _k_.

## Coverings and edge colorings

If a graph has a 3-edge-coloring, so does every covering graph of it: each edge is given the color of the edge that it corresponds to.
The reverse is not true.
For example, <Snark n={6} /> is a covering graph of <Snark n={3} />, and it has a 3-edge-coloring because 6 is even.
