## Three times around

Five and seven are prime, but nine is three threes, and that makes room for something new.
Number the blocks of <Snark n={9} /> around its ring as 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3, 1, 2, 3.
Now pretend that blocks with the same number are the same block.
What you're left with is [<Snark n={3} />](/g3).

<Highlight

	Every third block of <Snark n={9} /> lands on the same block of <Snark n={3} />.
</Highlight>

Graph theorists say that <Snark n={9} /> is a _triple cover_ of <Snark n={3} />.
Every vertex of the small graph has three above it in the large one, and walking anywhere in the large graph casts a shadow of a walk in the small one.
A lap around <Snark n={9} /> is three laps around <Snark n={3} />.

## Why coverings matter

If <Snark n={3} /> could be colored with three colors, then so could <Snark n={9} />: each edge would copy the color of the edge beneath it.
The reverse is not true in general.
Wrapping can make a graph easier to color: wrapping <Snark n={3} /> twice gives the six blocks of <Snark n={6} />, which three colors are enough for.
Wrapping it three times doesn't help, as three is odd.

The triangle of <Snark n={3} />, which [keeps it from being a snark](/g3) by most definitions, also comes undone along the way.
It unwinds into the nonagon at the center.
