## Is it a snark?

With only three blocks, the hub closes up into a triangle.
That triangle is the whole controversy.

<Highlight
	blocks={3}
	kind="hub"
	label="The Goldberg graph G3, with the triangle in its center emphasized."
>
	The hub of <Snark n={3} /> is its one triangle.
</Highlight>

The proof that three colors fail never asks how big the ring is, only whether it's odd.
So <Snark n={3} /> is as impossible to color as any of its larger siblings.
Where authors disagree is whether that's interesting enough:

- The [common definition of a snark](/#snark) has no cycles shorter than five, because they never make a cubic graph any harder to color.
  A triangle, for one, can be shrunk to a single vertex without changing the answer.
  By that standard the family starts at [five blocks](/g5) <Cite id="mohar2006" />.
- Others only ask that three colors fail, and that the graph doesn't fall apart by removing one edge.
  They count <Snark n={3} /> as the first Goldberg snark <Cite id="campos2011" />.

## What's inside of it

Shrink the triangle to a vertex, and the 22 vertices left do form a snark by anybody's definition.
It's one of the two smallest [Loupekine snarks](/#relatives), which were published in 1976, a few years before the Goldberg snarks <Cite id="berman2017" />.

There's one more distinction to having only three blocks.
Of all the graphs with pages here, this is the only one in which no vertex is more central than any other.
From anywhere, the farthest vertex is exactly four steps away.
