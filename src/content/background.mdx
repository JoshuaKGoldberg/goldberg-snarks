## Background

A _graph_ is a set of points, called vertices, joined by lines, called edges.
In a _cubic_ graph, three edges meet at every vertex.
A _cycle_ is a closed loop of edges that does not visit any vertex twice, and its _length_ is its number of edges.

An _edge coloring_ gives each edge of a graph a color, with different colors for edges that meet.
Most cubic graphs have an edge coloring with three colors, called a _3-edge-coloring_.
A _snark_ is a cubic graph that has no 3-edge-coloring and that meets [some other conditions](/#snark), such as having no triangle.

The [Goldberg snarks](/) are a family of snarks that Mark K. Goldberg published in 1981 <Cite id="goldberg1981" />.
The Goldberg snark <Snark n="k" /> is a ring of _k_ copies of a block, where _k_ is odd and at least 5.

A block (Figure 1) has eight vertices, labeled _a_ to _h_.
Those in block _i_ are labeled <Vertex i="i" role="a" /> to <Vertex i="i" role="h" />.
Three edges, called links, join each block to the next.
The links form part of three rings: the <Term is="outer">outer ring</Term>, the <Term is="middle">middle ring</Term>, and the <Term is="inner">inner ring</Term>.
